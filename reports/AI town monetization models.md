# Sell the simulation, not the town

Almost nobody makes money from an "AI town" as a town. Stanford's Smallville and a16z's AI Town are free research and starter-kit projects. Ten thousand GitHub stars later, there is still no known consumer product built on an AI Town fork that reports revenue. The real money in 2025-2026 sits in three places next to the town. First, **B2B "synthetic user" research**, where the Smallville team's own startup, Simile, raised about **$300M at a $2B valuation** and smaller vendors charge **$12.5K-$85K a year**. Second, **personality-driven AI entertainment**, where the money-maker is one AI character run by a human operator (Neuro-sama, Twitch's most-subscribed channel), not a town of agents. Third, **speculative crypto launchpads**, which collected tens of millions in trading fees while their tokens fell 85-99%. Cost is no longer the problem. A 25-agent town costs roughly **$0.10-$0.20 per agent per simulated day** on 2026's cheap models, against an implied $40-$100+ per agent-day in 2023. The hard parts are distribution, validity and retention. For an individual, the ranking is clear. Sell simulation-based research or build-for-hire services to businesses. Treat content and games as long shots. Template sales, hackathons and grants are pocket money or portfolio. Stay out of tokens unless you are the launchpad.

## The original towns earn nothing; the money went downstream

The category started with "Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior" (Park et al., UIST 2023): **25 agents in a Sims-like town called Smallville**. Its best-known emergent behavior was a Valentine's party that spread through the town by word of mouth ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.03442/)). a16z followed with AI Town, an MIT-licensed starter kit built on its portfolio company Convex's backend ([GitHub](https://github.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/)). AI Town has about **10.8k stars and 1.2k forks** as of November 2026 ([Star History](https://www.star-history.com/a16z-infra/ai-town)). Neither project has a revenue model, and the research found no commercial app or game built directly on an AI Town fork that reports revenue.

The value showed up elsewhere. For a16z and Convex, AI Town was developer marketing. Convex has since raised **$110.5M in total, including a $57M Series B in August 2026**, but as a general "AI-optimized application backend," not as a town company ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/04/convex-reels-57m-ai-optimized-application-backend/)). For the Stanford team, Smallville became credibility for a venture-backed research company. For crypto traders, it became a story to sell tokens with. The most famous "AI civilization" demo, Altera's Project Sid (1,000+ agents in Minecraft that formed economies and spread a religion), gives a clear signal: the team renamed itself Fundamental Research Labs, raised **$33M led by Prosus**, and now earns money from **Shortcut, an AI Excel analyst**, not from simulation ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/fundamental-research-labs-secures-33m-20250805); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/01/fundamental-research-labs-nabs-30-million-from-prosus-to-build-ai-agents-for-multiple-verticals)). Science Focus put it plainly: Project Sid "wasn't much of a product" ([Science Focus](https://www.sciencefocus.com/future-technology/ai-agents-village)).

### Running a town now costs pennies per agent-day

In 2023 the researchers said two simulated days of Smallville cost "thousands of dollars in ChatGPT tokens" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2023/04/11/sims_ai_generation/)). An open-source reproduction gives a measured baseline: **$18.50 for 25 agents over one full game day** on gpt-3.5-turbo-0125, or **$0.74 per agent-day** ([GitHub drudilorenzo/generative_agents](https://github.com/drudilorenzo/generative_agents)). Back-calculating from that figure gives about 1.2M tokens per agent-day, mostly input. The table applies that estimate to 2026 list prices, including Claude Haiku 5.5 at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens ([Claude pricing](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing)), GPT-5 nano at $0.05/$0.40 ([OpenAI](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/models/gpt-5-nano)), and Groq-hosted Llama 3.1 8B at about $0.05/$0.08 ([aipricing.guru](https://www.aipricing.guru/groq-pricing/)).

| Model (2026) | Est. cost per agent-day | 25-agent town per day | 25 agents for a year |
|---|---|---|---|
| Groq Llama 3.1 8B | ~$0.065 | ~$1.60 | ~$590 |
| GPT-5 nano | ~$0.10 | ~$2.60 | ~$950 |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 | ~$0.17 (~$0.10 with prompt caching) | ~$4.30 | ~$1,560 |
| GPT-5 mini | ~$0.52 | ~$13 | ~$4,730 |
| gpt-3.5-turbo (measured baseline) | $0.74 | $18.50 | ~$6,750 |
| Gemini 3.8 Flash (2026 promo price) | ~$1.28 | ~$32 | ~$11,700 |

All rows except the baseline are estimates. A further 57% saving is possible with the caching and policy-reuse techniques from Tencent's Affordable Generative Agents paper ([arXiv 2402.02053](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02053v2)). Reasoning models such as GPT-5 mini can bill hidden reasoning tokens that multiply their cost. A town fast-forwarded 10x costs 10x per wall-clock day. Hosting on Convex Pro costs **$25 per developer per month for 25M function calls** ([Convex pricing](https://www.convex.dev/pricing)), so a public 25-agent town comes to roughly **$150-$450 a month all-in** (estimate). That is cheap enough for a hobbyist, and it is still a bill with no revenue attached. The original AI Town even pauses itself after five minutes without a viewer to save money ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/a16z-infra/ai-town/main/README.md)). The practical upshot: compute fell roughly 200-1,000x, so whoever finds paying demand captures nearly all the margin, and no technical moat comes from running the town cheaply.

## B2B simulation is the only model with nine-figure checks

Selling simulated people to businesses is the category's one serious market. **Simile**, founded by Joon Sung Park of the Generative Agents team, came out of stealth in February 2026 with **$100M led by Index Ventures**. Five months later it raised **$200M at a $2B valuation** led by Greenoaks ([FinSMEs](https://www.finsmes.com/2026/07/simile-raises-200m-in-series-b-funding-at-2-billion-valuation.html); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/ai-startup-raised-300-million-040101685.html)). Named customers include CVS Health, Wealthfront and Gallup, which began validating Simile's agents against its probability panel in May 2026 ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/simile/); [Insight Innovation Ventures](https://insightinnovationventures.substack.com/p/simile-just-priced-the-calibration)). Simile does not disclose revenue. One analyst backs out about $100M ARR from an assumed 20x multiple ([angelinvestorsnetwork](https://angelinvestorsnetwork.com/venture-capital/simile-200m-series-b-behavioral-ai-greenoaks-2026)). That figure is an estimate, and customers named during stealth "often reflect pilot programs rather than committed revenue" ([implicator.ai](https://www.implicator.ai/the-stanford-team-that-invented-generative-agents-wants-to-kill-the-focus-group/)). **Aaru** raised a Redpoint-led Series A at a **$1B "headline" valuation** with **ARR under $10M** ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/12/05/ai-synthetic-research-startup-aaru-raised-a-series-a-at-a-1b-headline-valuation/)), which implies a revenue multiple above 100x. Aaru's track record is mixed: it says it called a 2024 New York primary within 371 votes ([CO/AI](https://getcoai.com/news/this-startup-uses-ai-agents-to-predict-election-outcomes-better-than-humans/)), and its 2024 presidential simulation reportedly had Harris winning.

Below the two leaders, prices are public and fall into three tiers.

| Tier | Example and price | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Self-serve SaaS | Ask Rally $20-$500/mo; SmartFocus $99/session or $149-$449/mo | [getminds.ai](https://getminds.ai/blog/synthetic-users-alternatives); [Agility PR](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-agency-news/smartfocus-ai-enables-everyone-to-run-ai-focus-groups-on-demand-and-in-minutes/) |
| Mid-market annual | Synthetic Users from $12,500/yr (older sheet: $18K-$85K) | [Synthetic Users](https://www.syntheticusers.com/pricing) |
| Pilot plus retainer | Lakmoos €10K one-month pilot, then from €4K/mo | [getminds.ai](https://getminds.ai/blog/synthetic-respondents-comparison-hub) |
| Enterprise custom | Simile, Aaru, Electric Twin ($14M raised), Evidenza: unpublished | [tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/02/12/electric-twin-expands-ai-audience-platform-with-14m-round/) |

The economics are what make this attractive to a small operator. A dev shop estimates **100 personas answering 10 questions costs $3-$5 in tokens** ([RapidDev](https://www.rapidevelopers.com/ai-implementation/ai-driven-virtual-focus-group-tool-ai-white-label)). A vendor comparison prices a traditional four-to-six-session focus-group project at **$24K-$90K**, against **$5K-$15K** for a synthetic study ([neuroflash](https://neuroflash.com/blog/roi/cost-comparison-synthetic-vs-traditional/)). The target is the fieldwork-heavy part of a **~$56B market-research sector** inside a ~$153B insights industry ([Research World/ESOMAR](https://researchworld.com/articles/inside-the-153bn-insights-industry)). Buyers are CPG brands, banks, healthcare companies, telcos, consultancies (EY, Accenture, Deloitte, Teneo) and political campaigns.

### The incumbents and the validity research both narrow the opening

Two forces shrink this opportunity. First, incumbents have bundled synthetic panels into existing subscriptions. Qualtrics Edge Audiences claims up to 70% lower survey costs ([Qualtrics](https://www.qualtrics.com/news/qualtrics-introduces-new-market-intelligence-capabilities-for-the-next-generation-of-market-insights/)). Toluna builds personas from its 19.4M-member US panel ([Research Live](https://www.research-live.com/article/news/toluna-releases-synthetic-personas-in-us/id/5135503)). YouGov bought Yabble for £4.5M upfront ([YouGov](https://business.yougov.com/content/50596-yougov-acquires-yabble)). Second, the science is shakier than the marketing. Simile's headline "85%" result means agents matched people's survey answers **85% as well as those people matched their own answers two weeks later**. It depended on **in-depth qualitative interviews with each of 1,052 real people** ([Stanford HAI](https://hai.stanford.edu/policy/simulating-human-behavior-with-ai-agents); [arXiv 2411.10109](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109)). Most commercial tools instead prompt with thin demographic descriptions, which the same paper showed to be more biased. Newer studies find that silicon samples "match the answers but not the respondents" ([arXiv 2610.30900](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30900)). Confidence intervals built from synthetic-only data **covered the true value in just 3% of tasks** ([arXiv 2606.13629](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13629)), and ESOMAR calls LLM responses "still too immature to replace fieldwork" ([Hamilton/ESOMAR](https://hamilton.global/en/global-research-software-2025-esomarreport/)). The honest product is directional screening: concept triage and message pre-tests. It is not market sizing or subgroup inference. The defensible moat is proprietary grounding data, such as a client's CRM records, past surveys or interviews. Persona prompting alone is a commodity.

Open-ended town or society simulations have not been sold this way. Tsinghua's AgentSociety and Berkeley's CivicSim are research tools, and **no venture-backed "synthetic citizens for government" vendor with disclosed funding** turned up ([Berkeley iSchool](https://www.ischool.berkeley.edu/programs/mims/projects/2026/civicsim-synthetic-personas-inclusive-policy-testing)). The companies that win sell narrow survey and interview prediction and call it simulation.

## Consumer entertainment pays one personality, not a town

The clearest consumer success is not a town. **Neuro-sama**, a single AI VTuber run by the developer Vedal987, set Twitch Hype Train records of **118,989 subs in December 2025** and became **Twitch's most-subscribed channel with 160k+ active subs** in January 2026, more than double the top human streamer's ~74k ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2026/01/05/neuro-sama-vedal987-most-subscribed-hype-train-record); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/twitchs-most-subscribed-streamer-is-not-human)). Revenue is undisclosed and described only as "life-changing." The multi-agent equivalent, **Nothing, Forever** (an endless AI Seinfeld), peaked at about **11,000 concurrent viewers**. It was then banned from Twitch for 14 days after its team switched to a cheaper, less-moderated model and a character performed a transphobic standup bit ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/02/06/ai-generated-seinfeld-suspended-on-twitch-for-ai-generated-transphobic-jokes/)). It ran on Patreon and never recovered its audience. That episode captures the core tension in 24/7 streaming: cheap inference raises moderation risk, and one bad output can end the channel.

Agent-simulation videos work mainly as marketing. Project Sid's Minecraft civilization and Emergent Garden's open-source Mindcraft bots went viral and fed a fundraise and a GitHub project ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2024/11/27/1107377/a-minecraft-town-of-ai-characters-made-friends-invented-jobs-and-spread-religion/); [UCI](https://sites.uci.edu/kolbynottingham/2024/10/30/mindcraft)). No ad or sponsor revenue figures exist for any agent-sim channel. The human-played version of the genre is large, though: the creator Ish has several Minecraft "civilization" videos above 10M views ([The Science Survey](https://thesciencesurvey.com/spotlight/2026/03/08/the-rise-of-minecraft-civilization-videos/)). That suggests an AI-agent version can find an audience if it is edited and story-driven rather than streamed raw.

In games, AI societies sell as a feature, not as the product. Krafton's **inZOI** sold **more than 1M copies in its first Early Access week** (March 2025), helped by its "Smart Zoi" AI NPCs ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/life-sim/kraftons-first-stab-at-a-life-sim-game-is-going-swimmingly-as-inzoi-managed-to-shift-over-1-million-copies-in-its-first-week/)). Concurrent players then fell about **90% from an 80k peak**, and Smart Zoi is still "experimental" because it needs too much compute, with full integration pushed to H2 2026 or later ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/inzoi-player-count-falling-publisher-comment/); [HappyGamer](https://happygamer.com/inzoi-update-september-2026/)). At indie scale, the AI-NPC voice game **Suck Up!** made an estimated **~5.6K copies and ~$57.6K gross** at $16.99 ([Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/2726370/Suck_Up)). Fable's **Showrunner**, the "Netflix of AI" that grew out of its AI South Park agent simulation, raised Amazon Alexa Fund money (about $50M per PitchBook, unconfirmed). It sells creator credits at a reported **$10-$40/month** and had ~10k alpha users with a 100k+ waitlist ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/startup-behind-unauthorized-ai-south-park-episodes-raises-50-million-from-amazon-to-top-latest-fundings); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/charliefink/2025/07/30/amazon-backs-showrunners-ai-streaming-platform-as-it-launches-satirical-series-exit-valley/)). No 2026 traction data turned up. Inworld, the NPC-engine supplier valued above $500M in 2023 on more than $100M raised, has estimated revenue of only **$1-10M** and **cut its prices by more than 50% in June 2026** ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/companies/inworld-ai/); [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260610968386/en/)). That points to an AI-character layer that is turning into a commodity.

## Crypto paid the launchpads and burned everyone else

Tokens produced the biggest numbers in this space and the least durable value. The AI-agent token sector peaked around **2 January 2025**. **ai16z/ElizaOS**, a token named as a parody of a16z, hit **$2.39B** in market cap. By August 2026 its founder had declared the token "dead" after a Burwick Law class action, which alleged among other things that no autonomous AI actually governed the fund, was settled with the remaining treasury ([CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/08/05/ai-agent-token-once-worth-usd2-4-billion-ends-with-founder-calling-it-dead); [Burwick Law](https://www.burwick.law/insights/burwick-law-files-class-action-over-ai16z-and-elizaos-tokens)). **Virtuals Protocol** survives at about **$283M**, down roughly 91-94% from a peak price of $5.07 ([Coinbase](https://www.coinbase.com/price/virtual-protocol)). **AIXBT** is down about 98%, **Freysa** about 96% and **MyShell** about 95% ([CoinGecko](https://www.coingecko.com/en/coins/aixbt-by-virtuals); [DeFiLlama](https://defillama.com/token/fai); [KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/price/SHELL)). The "AI world" tokens were even shorter-lived. Moltbook, a social network for AI agents, claimed 1.6M agents in January 2026 and collapsed within about four to eight days, while the loosely linked MOLT token spiked to around $100M and then fell 60-75% ([DL News](https://dlnews.com/articles/markets/what-is-moltbook-base-token-tied-to-ai-bot-forum-crashes/)). Copycat "AI Town" tokens exist with no connection to a16z ([DexScreener](https://dexscreener.com/solana/3mnazsjrmgiz7jvvwe6icw4uscg4zjfefw2gmaexzxvi)).

The cash went to whoever took a cut of trades. Virtuals has earned **$74.3M in lifetime fees**, more than half of it in Q4 2024 and Q1 2025. Its September 2026 fees were **$566K, about 3% of the January 2025 peak month** ([ChainWard](https://chainward.ai/decodes/virtuals-top-dog)). One November 2026 review puts trailing 30-day revenue at **about $57K**, against a market cap near $390M ([CryptoDailyAlert](https://cryptodailyalert.com/virtuals-review-2026)). Its much-promoted agent-commerce "aGDP" of $479M is not revenue, and it is propped up by a **$1M-a-month incentive program** ([Forkast](https://forkast.news/the-machine-as-buyer-virtuals-acp-and-the-evaluator-problem/); [TechIntelPro](https://techintelpro.com/news/ai/agentic-ai/virtuals-protocol-launches-revenue-network-for-ai-agent-commerce)). Pump.fun, the general launchpad behind most Solana agent memecoins, peaked at $6.7M a day in revenue in January 2025 and fell 96% by August ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/370812/pump-fun-surpasses-hyperliquid-revenue)). On the same launchpads, **76,469 of 100,063 Solana tokens launched in H1 2025 were flagged as likely rug pulls** ([arXiv 2603.24625](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1)). SEC staff say memecoins are not securities ([SEC](https://sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/staff-statement-meme-coins)), so buyers have little federal protection. For builders, the main legal risk is private litigation that can empty a treasury without any judgment. For a builder, a token is a one-time liquidity event with a half-life of weeks. Profit in it depends on selling to later buyers, which is the scam dynamic itself.

## Services, templates, grants and sponsorships pay small but real money

Freelance work on agent systems has clear prices, and they are modest. Upwork postings for multi-agent work in LangGraph, CrewAI or AutoGen pay **$25-$45/hr** ([Upwork](https://www.upwork.com/jobs/~022066934148697692363); [Upwork agency](https://www.upwork.com/agencies/2103936255231378932/)). Multi-agent skills reportedly add 20-30% to base rates ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/jahanzaibai/ai-automation-consultant-hourly-rate-what-youll-actually-pay-in-2026-and-when-its-worth-it-55ln)). One three-month gig at $30/hr for 30 hours a week grosses about **$11.7K**, which would pay for roughly seven years of Haiku-class inference for a 25-agent town (estimate). Templates are a thin niche. No AI-town-specific product turned up on Gumroad. Adjacent agent kits sell for **$19.99-$29**, and an agent-simulation course bundle for **$345** ([Gumroad](https://arsiliath.gumroad.com/l/bundle); [Gumroad](https://chongdashu.gumroad.com/l/adk-builder-pack-1)). Because AI Town itself is free and MIT-licensed, a paid template has to sell setup, customization or teaching, not code.

Hackathons and grants build a portfolio rather than income. A typical college prize is **$750** (Hack@Brown 2026's Fetch.ai track), and the largest pools are crypto-sponsored, such as Colosseum's **$100K** Solana agent hackathon ([Fetch.ai](https://innovationlab.fetch.ai/events/hack-brown-2026); [Colosseum](https://colosseum.com/agent-hackathon/)). A UCL team won Anthropic's Claude Agent SDK prize with a 100-agent pixel-art office simulation, which shows that AI-town demos still win judges ([EdTech Innovation Hub](https://www.edtechinnovationhub.com/news/ucl-team-wins-anthropic-prize-after-simulating-hackathon-with-ai-agents)). The a16z Open Source AI Grant is non-dilutive but **invitation-only**, with no public application ([a16z](https://a16z.com/supporting-the-open-source-ai-community/)). The open-source frameworks (Microsoft TinyTroupe, DeepMind Concordia, CAMEL's OASIS, AI Town) charge nothing. CAMEL funds itself through GitHub Sponsors plus 10% of its Eigent subscription product ([Eigent](https://www.eigent.ai/es/open-source-fund)). As for **sponsorships**, the research found **no documented brand-sponsored AI town or sponsor deal for an agent-sim channel**. Anyone selling that is creating the category, not entering one.

## Ranked: where an individual should actually spend effort

The ranking below weighs proven willingness to pay, margin, time to first dollar and downside. Revenue ranges for an individual are estimates inferred from the price anchors above, not observed operator earnings.

| Rank | Path | Evidence of demand | Realistic individual upside | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Boutique synthetic-research studies for one vertical (pilot plus retainer, grounded in client data) | Simile $2B, Aaru <$10M ARR, $12.5K-$85K/yr list prices, $3-$5 compute per study | $2K-$15K per study; ~$4K/mo retainers | Validity backlash; Qualtrics/Toluna bundling |
| 2 | Freelance or agency builds of agent simulations and multi-agent systems | Steady Upwork demand at $25-$45/hr | ~$10K per contract; scales with rate and reputation | Low rates; template-grade competition |
| 3 | Narrow self-serve simulation SaaS | Ask Rally, SmartFocus at $20-$500/mo | Small MRR unless distribution is solved | Crowded; commoditized persona prompting |
| 4 | AI-personality content (one character, human-operated, heavily edited) | Neuro-sama 160k+ subs; Minecraft sim genre at 10M+ views | Power law: near zero or life-changing | Moderation incidents; novelty decay |
| 5 | Game or app where agent NPCs are a feature | inZOI 1M week-one sales; Suck Up! ~$57.6K gross | Indie scale tens of thousands of dollars | Retention; compute limits on AI depth |
| 6 | Templates and courses | $19.99-$345 adjacent products | Hundreds to low thousands per month | Free MIT-licensed original |
| 7 | Hackathons, grants, open-source sponsorship | $750 typical prize; invite-only a16z grants | Portfolio and leads, not income | Opportunity cost |
| 8 | Launching a token for your town | Launchpad fees collapsed 96-97%; 76% of Solana launches flagged as rugs | One-time extraction | Class actions; reputational ruin |

## Conclusion

The AI town proved to be a demonstration of a capability, and the paying markets formed around the capability rather than the demo. Simulated people sell to businesses when they are framed as faster, cheaper research. AI characters sell to audiences when they have one personality and a human editor. The open-ended town in between has no buyer. The right mental model is "the town is a rendering layer for a simulation that someone already wants answered." The questions with buyers are how customers will react, how voters will split, and what will happen next in the story. A pixel-art map adds little unless it makes the answer more persuasive in a client deck or more watchable in an edited video.

Two shifts will decide the next two years. Inference costs have fallen by two to three orders of magnitude, so the barrier to entry is gone and margins will go to whoever owns grounding data or an audience. Gallup's independent validation of Simile, along with the growing academic critique of silicon samples, will either legitimize synthetic research as a budget line or cap it at early-stage screening. The pragmatic bet is to build hybrid human-plus-synthetic research offerings for a vertical you know. That way the business survives either verdict, and the "AI town" becomes the demo that wins the meeting rather than the product you hope someone pays for.
