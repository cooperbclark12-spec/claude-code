# Crypto/Token Monetization of AI-Agent Towns and AI-Agent Worlds

Research date: 2026-11-21. Method note: WebFetch was blocked for most hosts in this session (coindesk.com, cryptodailyalert.com did not resolve), so nearly all findings come from search-result snippets and summaries, not full-page reads. Many market-data numbers come from aggregator or AI-generated analysis pages (CoinStats AI, CMC AI, DexTools guides). Treat every price/market-cap number as a dated snapshot. Where sources conflict, both are given.

## 1. Which projects tokenized AI agents / agent worlds, and what happened to their valuations (peak vs. now)?

### Takeaway
The "AI agent token" boom peaked around 2 January 2025 and then collapsed. The large platform tokens are down about 85-99% from their peaks. The flagship ai16z/ElizaOS token was declared "dead" by its founder in August 2026 after a class-action settlement used up the treasury. Virtuals Protocol survives at roughly $280-390M market cap (Nov 2026), down from about $5B. Agent "world" experiments (Moltbook, Freysa, Colony) produced short speculative spikes rather than lasting token value.

## 2. Revenue to teams and creators (launchpad fees, token sales, treasury), as distinct from token price

### Takeaway
Real cash revenue is small relative to peak valuations and was concentrated in the Q4 2024-Q1 2025 trading mania. Virtuals' lifetime fees of about $74M (more than half earned in two quarters) compare with a peak value of about $5B. Its monthly fees fell to about $0.57M in Sep 2026, about 3% of the Jan 2025 peak month. The most profitable entity in the stack is the generic launchpad (pump.fun), whose revenue comes from memecoin trading in general, not from agents.

### Cited Findings
- **Virtuals fee model:** Each agent-token trade carries a 1% fee. Sources disagree on the split (70% creator / 30% treasury, versus 100% to treasury, then split between creator, treasury and buyback-and-burn). Agent creation is now free (formerly 100 VIRTUAL) — [Search summary: datawallet / gate.com / ventureburn](https://www.datawallet.com/crypto/what-is-virtuals-protocol)
- **Virtuals lifetime fees:** $74.32M all-time fees on DefiLlama, with about $41.7M retained by the protocol. Q4 2024 plus Q1 2025 produced about $40.7M, more than half of the total. Sep 2026 protocol fees were about $566K, about 3% of the Jan 2025 peak month ($16.59M) — [ChainWard decode](https://chainward.ai/decodes/virtuals-top-dog); [Blockworks Virtuals income statement](https://blockworks.com/analytics/virtuals/virtuals-financials-new/virtuals-income-statement-2)
- Other Virtuals revenue figures conflict: a 30-day revenue of $57,373 against a $390M market cap, "ACP total revenue 4.53M", and the note that "aGDP is not revenue" — [CryptoDailyAlert, Nov 2026](https://cryptodailyalert.com/virtuals-review-2026). Also cited: $39.5M in cumulative protocol revenue, and $75M+ "cumulative protocol revenue" reported Feb 2026. Definitions differ — [Forkast](https://forkast.news/the-machine-as-buyer-virtuals-acp-and-the-evaluator-problem/)
- An Oct 2026 analysis found "no verified breakdown" of how much fee activity becomes value for VIRTUAL holders — [CoinStats](https://coinstats.app/ai/a/investment-analysis-virtual-protocol)
- At one point daily Virtuals ecosystem fees rose from $33K to $230K while market cap fell 10% — [Blockworks](https://blockworks.co/news/ai-agent-market-wipeout)
- **Pump.fun (the general launchpad behind GOAT and most Solana agent memecoins):** revenue comes from token-creation, liquidity and trading fees — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/366783/pumps-average-token-buyback-price-floats-40-above-spot-market-even-as-daily-revenue-trends-lower)
  - Revenue fell 96%, from a $6.7M/day peak in Jan 2025 to $206,059 on Aug 1, 2025. It rebounded to $3.38M/day in mid-Sept 2025, briefly topping Hyperliquid — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/370812/pump-fun-surpasses-hyperliquid-revenue)
  - From July 2025 it sent 100% of revenue to PUMP buybacks. Reported cumulative buybacks: about $94.5M by early Sept 2025, then $205M+, then $400M+ "as token stays flat" — [crypto.news](https://crypto.news/pump-fun-spends-nearly-95m-on-token-buybacks/); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/defi/pump-fun-s-pump-buybacks-top-usd400m-as-token-stays-flat)
  - In April 2026 it burned about $370M of PUMP (about 36% of circulating supply) and moved to buybacks funded by 50% of net revenue — [CoinDesk, Apr 29, 2026](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/04/29/pump-fun-burns-36-of-pump-supply-in-usd370-million-wipe-locks-50-revenue-into-ongoing-buybacks)
  - Competitor LetsBonk overtook pump.fun on 24h revenue on Jul 7, 2025 — [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/post/368584/solana-launchpad-pump-fun-buys-back-15-million-in-native-tokens-over-past-two-weeks)
- **ai16z/ElizaOS treasury:** The settlement transferred all remaining treasury funds to holders/plaintiffs, leaving no capital. The amount was not disclosed — [The Block](https://theblock.co/post/410774/eliza-labs-native-token-dead); [Decrypt](https://decrypt.co/374958/eliza-ai-token-dead-shuts-down-foundation-lawsuit)
- **Truth Terminal:** the bot's GOAT holdings were worth more than $500K (2024) — [Coingape](https://coingape.com/truth-terminal-becomes-first-ai-crypto-millionaire-as-goat-crosses-400m/)
- **Freysa:** the team keeps 20-30% of message fees, part of it spent on buybacks. Prize payouts are small (Act I $47,148) — [iq.wiki](https://iq.wiki/en/wiki/freysa-ai)
- **Venture and exchange funding:** Parallel raised $35M — [games.gg](https://games.gg/es/news/parallel-studios-anuncia-ronda-de-financiacion-de-35-millones/). Spectral raised a $7M seed — [DexTools](https://www.dextools.io/tutorials/what-is-spectral-network-spec-onchain-ai-agents-guide-2026). Holoworld's distribution came via a Binance airdrop plus a 7M HOLO trading promo — [Binance Research](https://www.binance.com/research/projects/holoworld-ai)

### Inferences
- Value capture comes in layers. Launchpads earn per-trade fees no matter what happens to the price. Creators and insiders earn mainly by holding or selling allocations near the top, plus a small trading-fee share. Late buyers fund both. Fee revenue is a function of speculative volume, so it falls together with price (Virtuals monthly fees down about 97%; pump.fun daily revenue down 96% at its 2025 trough).
- Rough ratio, my estimate: Virtuals' peak market cap (about $5B) was roughly 65x its lifetime fees (about $74M), and its current market cap (about $280-390M) is roughly 40-60x annualized recent fees (about $0.57M x 12, about $7M). It remains richly valued on fees even after the crash.
- pump.fun's large buybacks ($400M+) produced a "flat" token. Even real revenue does not guarantee holder returns when unlock and emission supply is large.

### Gaps
- No audited revenue for any of these teams. Creator-level earnings (median Virtuals agent creator income) were not found.
- No data on NFT or subscription revenue for agent worlds specifically.

## 3. How individual builders profit; share of rugs and pump-and-dumps; regulatory actions

### Takeaway
The main builder playbook is launching a token (on pump.fun or Virtuals) and profiting from allocation sales and trading-fee shares, not from product revenue. On Solana, about 76% of tokens launched in H1 2025 were flagged as likely rug pulls. US regulators classed most memecoins as outside securities law (SEC staff, Feb 2025), so remedies have come mainly through private class actions (ai16z) rather than enforcement. The CFTC was still at the discussion stage on AI-agent liability in Oct 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Rug-pull prevalence (all Solana tokens, not AI-specific):** 76,469 of 100,063 tokens launched on Solana DEXes in H1 2025 were flagged as likely rugs, with at least $151M in direct losses across 7,322 profitable addresses, and an audit false-positive rate of about 0.26%. This is a non-peer-reviewed preprint using behavioral labels — [arXiv 2603.24625 (SolRugDetector)](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.24625v1); [The Factum](https://thefactum.ai/articles/dc285ec8-0801-474e-a97d-16ae1befffb6)
- A figure of $2.8B in 2025 rug-pull losses is attributed to Chainalysis by a secondary blog; it could not be verified — [Deepstrike](https://deepstrike.io/blog/rug-pull-statistics)
- Single-case anecdote: an AI trading agent bought a token that rugged 94% within 20 minutes, losing $12K (vendor blog, unverified) — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/amarodeabreu/your-ai-agent-is-one-rug-pull-away-from-losing-everything-heres-the-fix-3274)
- **Misattribution and impersonation pattern:** GOAT was launched by a third party and promoted by the bot — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/ai-bot-didnt-launch-goat-memecoin-did-promote-it). MOLT's link to Moltbook was unconfirmed. Moltbook's creator previously ran the Yesnoerror token project — [DL News](https://dlnews.com/articles/markets/what-is-moltbook-base-token-tied-to-ai-bot-forum-crashes/); [Frank on Fraud](https://frankonfraud.com/ai-agents-built-social-network-then-scammers-wrecked-it/)
- **ai16z class action:** Burwick Law filed in SDNY on Apr 22, 2026 (Case 1:26-cv-03238), alleging false advertising, deceptive practices, negligent misrepresentation and unjust enrichment. It alleges the project was not actually governed by an autonomous AI agent, and that a token migration diluted holders by giving 40% of newly minted tokens to defendant-controlled entities (allegations, not findings). The case settled in Aug 2026 for the remaining treasury; the founder called the claims meritless but said they were unaffordable to fight — [Burwick Law](https://www.burwick.law/insights/burwick-law-files-class-action-over-ai16z-and-elizaos-tokens); [The Defiant](https://thedefiant.io/news/tokens/eliza-labs-shaw-walters-says-ai16z-token-is-dead-after-settling-burwick-law-class-action); [CryptoTimes](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/08/05/elizaos-founder-declares-token-completely-dead-after-burwick-class-action-settlement/)
- **SEC:** a Division of Corporation Finance staff statement (Feb 27, 2025) says the types of meme coins it describes are not securities, so buyers lack federal securities-law protection. It is non-binding staff guidance — [SEC](https://sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/staff-statement-meme-coins); [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/sitecore/content/whlaunch/home/research/news-publications/20250313-the-state-of-meme-coin-regulation-sec-staffs-statement-and-other-considerations); [Harvard Law Forum](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2025/03/19/implications-of-the-secs-stance-that-meme-coins-are-not-securities/)
- Under Chair Atkins the SEC sharply reduced crypto enforcement and focuses on large fraud. "AI-washing" cases continue (Delphia, Global Predictions, Presto Automation), but none involve agent tokens — [Secretariat](https://secretariat-intl.com/insights/what-companies-should-be-watching-in-securities-enforcement-for-the-remainder-of-2026/)
- SEC-CFTC MOU signed Mar 11, 2026 — [Willkie](https://www.willkie.com/publications/2026/07/crypto-coordination-takes-shape-an-in-depth-review-of-the-landmark-sec-and-cftc-joint-release)
- **CFTC:** in Oct 2026, Chair Mike Selig warned that AI agents launching memecoins or staking ETH create a "liability gap", questioned whether agent developers must register under the CEA, and floated "Know Your Agent" (KYA). He cited an AI autonomously creating the LUM memecoin — [Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/a-fridge-that-launches-memecoins-cftc-floats-ai-crypto-future)
- No SEC or CFTC enforcement action specifically against an AI-agent token was found for 2025-2026 — [The Block 2026 regulation outlook](https://www.theblock.co/post/383241/crypto-regulation-2026-sec-ambitious-agenda-empowered-cftc)

### Inferences
- The fact that 76% of all Solana launches are likely rugs is a reasonable prior for low-cap AI-agent tokens on the same launchpads. No AI-specific rate is measured, so any AI-specific figure is an estimate.
- With the SEC stepping back, private litigation (Burwick has specialized in memecoin cases) is the main legal risk for teams. It can wipe a treasury even without a judgment.

### Gaps
- No dataset isolates rug or pump-and-dump rates for AI-agent tokens specifically.
- No DOJ criminal cases tied to AI-agent tokens were found.

## 4. Is there any sustainable non-speculative revenue?

### Takeaway
Little, and none of it is verified. Virtuals' Agent Commerce Protocol (ACP) reports "aGDP" of about $479M and 1.77M jobs (Mar 2026), but critics note that aGDP is not revenue, and that ACP is propped up by an incentive program paying up to $1M/month. Protocol revenue still appears to come mostly from token launches and trading. Pay-to-play agent games (Freysa) generate small fee flows. No agent-town project showed meaningful subscription or product revenue.

### Cited Findings
- As of Mar 2026, Virtuals ACP reported total aGDP of $479M, 1.77M+ completed jobs and 18,000+ deployed agents. The 2026 goal is to take aGDP from more than $300M to $3B+ annualized — [BlockEden](https://blockeden.xyz/blog/2026/04/04/virtuals-protocol-ai-economic-operating-system-on-chain-agent-economy/)
- The Revenue Network (launched Feb 2026, Consensus HK) distributes up to $1M/month to agents that sell services via ACP. This is a subsidy, not organic revenue — [TechIntelPro](https://techintelpro.com/news/ai/agentic-ai/virtuals-protocol-launches-revenue-network-for-ai-agent-commerce); [Virtuals Substack](https://virtuals.substack.com/p/virtuals-monthly-update-september)
- Critique: if Virtuals' $39.5M of protocol revenue comes mainly from launch infrastructure rather than ACP jobs, "Virtuals looks more like a launchpad." aGDP "needs independent verification" separating real commerce from protocol activity — [Forkast](https://forkast.news/the-machine-as-buyer-virtuals-acp-and-the-evaluator-problem/)
- 30-day Virtuals revenue was about $57K (Nov 2026 review) — [CryptoDailyAlert](https://cryptodailyalert.com/virtuals-review-2026)
- OKX Ventures (Mar 2026): daily agent-economy transaction counts were down sharply from the Dec peak, with infrastructure categories down more than 80% (machine-read from a Chinese source) — [iTiger / OKX Ventures](https://www.itiger.com/hant/news/2621935017)
- Freysa's message-fee model is a real but small cash flow, mostly recycled into the prize pool — [iq.wiki](https://iq.wiki/en/wiki/freysa-ai)
- Commentary contrasting dying agent tokens with Bittensor's model — [niteagent.com](https://niteagent.com/blog/why-agent-tokens-keep-dying-elizaos-bittensor/) (opinion blog)

### Inferences
- The only consistent cash-generating business in this space is taking a cut of speculative trading (launchpad fees). Agent-to-agent commerce (ACP) is the main claimed non-speculative stream, but it is incentive-subsidized and unaudited. A $57K/month run-rate (if correct) is negligible.
- For an "AI town" product, these results suggest that tokenizing the town gives a one-time liquidity event (fees plus allocation sales) with a short half-life. Recurring revenue would have to come from conventional sources (subscriptions, game sales, B2B), and none of the surveyed crypto projects has demonstrated these at scale.

### Gaps
- No independent audit of ACP job revenue, and no breakdown of how many ACP jobs are paid by real external customers versus wash or incentive activity.
- No subscription revenue data for MyShell, Holoworld or Spectral was found.
