## 1. Companies selling agent/persona simulation to businesses and governments: funding, valuation, revenue, pricing, customers

### Takeaway
By late 2026 the category had two capital-heavy leaders. Simile, the startup from the Stanford Generative Agents team, has raised about $300M and was valued at $2B in July 2026. Aaru raised a Series A in December 2025 at a $1B "headline" valuation, with ARR reported under $10M. Below them sits a long tail of seed-stage startups ($0.5M–$14M rounds) selling at prices from $20/month self-serve up to $12.5K–$85K+/year enterprise contracts. Incumbent panel companies (Qualtrics, Toluna, YouGov through Yabble) have added synthetic panels to existing subscriptions. Disclosed revenue is almost nonexistent: no company has published audited ARR.

## 2. Evidence on validity: Park et al. 2024 "1,000 people" and critiques of silicon sampling

### Takeaway
The headline 85% is a *normalized* figure. Agents predicted participants' General Social Survey answers 85% as accurately as the participants reproduced their own answers two weeks later; it is not an 85% raw match. Since 2025 a growing body of arXiv/SSRN work finds that silicon samples reproduce aggregate distributions reasonably well but are too narrow, biased for some subgroups, and poor at covering individual respondents. Statistical inference from synthetic-only data is badly miscalibrated.

### Cited Findings
- "Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People" (Park et al., arXiv 2411.10109, 15 Nov 2024). Agents were built from qualitative interviews with 1,052 real US individuals and benchmarked on the GSS, the Big Five, five behavioral-economics games and experimental replications. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109)
- The 85% figure is normalized accuracy: agents replicated GSS answers "85% as accurately as participants replicate their own answers two weeks later." — [Stanford HAI policy brief](https://hai.stanford.edu/policy/simulating-human-behavior-with-ai-agents)
- Interview-based agents reduced accuracy biases across racial and ideological groups compared with agents given only demographic descriptions. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109)
- Popular press framed it as "The End of Focus Groups? AI Replicates Consumer Responses With 85% Accuracy," which overstates the result. — [PYMNTS, 2024](https://www.pymnts.com/artificial-intelligence-2/2024/the-end-of-focus-groups-ai-replicates-consumer-responses-with-85percent-accuracy/)
- "Silicon Samples Match the Answers but Not the Respondents" (arXiv 2610.30900, Oct 2026) proposes a respondent coverage gap metric and finds that all six LLMs tested leave human respondents uncovered on two US social surveys. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30900)
- Cross-survey transfer paper: Argyle et al. (the original "silicon sampling") assessed distributional correspondence, not individual prediction; LLM response distributions are systematically narrower than human ones. — [arXiv 2607.03091](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.03091)
- "Plausible but Not Valid: A Psychometric Audit of LLMs as Synthetic Survey Respondents" (arXiv 2608.14606). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14606)
- Valid-inference paper: naive synthetic-only confidence intervals are far too narrow and biased, covering the truth in only 3% of tasks. — [arXiv 2606.13629](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13629)
- Four-level validity framework: LLM datasets preserve reliability and many structural relationships, but consumer responses are inflated and organizational responses attenuated relative to human baselines. — [SSRN 5835122, "The Silicon Sample"](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5835122) (attribution of this specific finding to this paper is from a search summary; verify)
- Re-fielding study (secondary write-up): more sophisticated generation improved silicon samples' ability to reproduce *themselves* far more than their agreement with humans (Prolific benchmark against 21 synthetic datasets from ChatGPT, Qwen and Gemini). — [190 Research Substack](https://190researchblog.substack.com/p/ai-survey)
- Singapore study: silicon sampling is more accurate for young, male, Chinese, Christian personas and much worse for Malay respondents aged 35–44. — [SSRN 7362005](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7362005) (attribution from search summary)
- ESOMAR's Global Research Software 2025 report calls LLM-generated responses "still too immature to replace fieldwork" and treats synthetic data as a complementary layer. — [Hamilton summary of ESOMAR report](https://hamilton.global/en/global-research-software-2025-esomarreport/)
- Vendor accuracy claims are self-reported and use undefined metrics: Evidenza 88% similarity; Lakmoos >98%; Qualtrics claims fine-tuned models beat general LLMs. — [listenlabs](https://listenlabs.com/articles/best-synthetic-respondent-platforms-compared/); [Qualtrics](https://www.qualtrics.com/articles/strategy-research/synthetic-research-breakthrough/)
- Gallup is running independent validation of Simile agents against its probability panel (announced May 2026; results not found). — [Insight Innovation Ventures](https://insightinnovationventures.substack.com/p/simile-just-priced-the-calibration)
- The Decision Lab on when synthetic focus groups work and when they mislead. — [The Decision Lab](https://thedecisionlab.com/insights/technology/focus-simulated-the-promise-and-limitations-of-ai-market-research)

### Inferences
- The strongest validity evidence (Park et al.) depends on hours of per-person interview data. Most commercial products use thin demographic prompts, the condition the same paper shows is more biased. Vendors citing "85%" while selling demographic personas are borrowing credibility.
- Evidence supports use for directional, early-stage screening (concept triage, message pre-tests) and does not support replacing fielded samples for sizing, subgroup analysis or inference. Qualtrics' own data shows synthetic adopters skew toward early-stage innovation.
- An independent third-party validation such as Gallup's could become a key sales asset or a key risk for the category.

### Gaps
- Big Five and economic-game accuracy figures from Park et al. were not captured in snippets.
- Gallup/Simile validation results are not published or found.
- No peer-reviewed head-to-head test of commercial vendors (Aaru vs Simile vs Synthetic Users) was found.
- No direct failed replication of Argyle et al. 2023 was found.

## 3. Market size: the research industry, the synthetic share, and who buys

### Takeaway
ESOMAR sizes the global insights industry at about $153B in 2024, including about $56B of core market research. A 2026 edition reportedly puts 2025 at about $165B. No authoritative figure exists for synthetic data's share. Vendor surveys report high claimed usage: Qualtrics says 69% of researchers used synthetic responses in the prior year (2024 survey). Buyers are CPG and consumer brands, financial services, healthcare/pharmacy, telecom and media, consultancies and agencies (EY, Accenture, Deloitte, Ogilvy, Teneo) and political campaigns.

## 4. Smaller operators: agencies, consultancies and freelancers selling simulation-based research, and how they price

### Takeaway
No published benchmark for freelance or agency synthetic-research fees was found. Observable price anchors are vendor list prices: $99/session and $149–$449/month (SmartFocus); $20–$500/month (Ask Rally); a €10K pilot (Lakmoos). There are also vendor-estimated synthetic studies of $5K–$15K versus $24K–$90K for traditional four-to-six-session focus group projects. Raw compute cost is a few dollars per study, so small operators sell persona design, calibration, interpretation and the client relationship.

### Cited Findings
- SmartFocus.ai: from $99 per AI focus group; subscriptions $149–$449/month. — [Agility PR](https://www.agilitypr.com/pr-agency-news/smartfocus-ai-enables-everyone-to-run-ai-focus-groups-on-demand-and-in-minutes/)
- Neuroflash (vendor) estimate: traditional focus group project of 4–6 sessions costs $24,000–$90,000; synthetic studies about $5,000–$15,000. — [neuroflash cost comparison](https://neuroflash.com/blog/roi/cost-comparison-synthetic-vs-traditional/)
- Dev-shop estimate: 100 personas × 10 questions costs about $3–$5 in LLM tokens (Claude Sonnet 4.6). The firm sells white-label virtual focus-group builds. — [RapidDev](https://www.rapidevelopers.com/ai-implementation/ai-driven-virtual-focus-group-tool-ai-white-label)
- Large agencies and consultancies productize this work:
  - WPP/Satalia "PersonAI" is listed on the Microsoft marketplace. — [Microsoft Marketplace](https://marketplace.microsoft.com/marketplace/apps/wpp1586249436761.personai_by_satalia)
  - Course5 Intelligence lists a "Synthetic Audiences" consulting service. — [Microsoft Marketplace](https://marketplace.microsoft.com/en-us/marketplace/consulting-services/course5intelligencepvtltd1592222720657.synthetic_audiences)
- Lakmoos' pilot-then-retainer model (a €10K one-month pilot, then from €4K/month) suits boutique sales. — [getminds.ai](https://getminds.ai/blog/synthetic-respondents-comparison-hub)
- Evidenza sells a managed service (a go-to-market plan in about 72 hours) rather than software seats. — [listenlabs](https://listenlabs.com/articles/best-synthetic-respondent-platforms-compared/)
- AI customer research pricing models for 2026 (vendor overview). — [listenlabs pricing article](https://listenlabs.ai/articles/ai-customer-research-pricing-2026)

### Inferences
- Plausible freelancer or boutique pricing is project-based, roughly $2K–$15K per synthetic study, anchored below traditional focus groups. Retainers fall in the €/$4K/month range. **This is inferred from vendor anchors, not observed freelancer rate cards.**
- Margins are high on compute, but differentiation is thin. Small operators likely need proprietary or client data (CRM, past surveys) or hybrid human-plus-synthetic offerings to justify fees, which matches the hybrid approach Digiday reports for agencies.

### Gaps
- No Upwork/Fiverr or freelancer pricing data was found.
- No agency fee disclosures were found for Ogilvy, WPP or others.
- No data on how many small consultancies sell synthetic research.
