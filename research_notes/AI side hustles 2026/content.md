## YouTube Partner Program requirements and the 2025-2026 "inauthentic / mass-produced content" policy changes

### Takeaway
To earn ad revenue on YouTube you still need 1,000 subscribers plus either 4,000 long-form watch hours in 12 months or 10M Shorts views in 90 days. Since July 2025, mass-produced or templated content, which is what most AI "slop" is, cannot be monetized, and YouTube can demonetize the whole channel. Enforcement grew a lot in 2026: big channels were removed, and Google describes an automated system that terminated about 130k spam channels in six months.

### Cited Findings
- YPP ad-revenue thresholds: 1,000 subscribers plus 4,000 valid public watch hours in 12 months, or 1,000 subscribers plus 10M valid public Shorts views in 90 days. Shorts-feed views do not count toward watch hours. You also need 2-Step Verification, a linked AdSense account and no active Community Guidelines strikes. — [YouTube Help: YPP overview](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- There is a lower 500-subscriber tier: 3 uploads in 90 days plus either 3,000 watch hours or 3M Shorts views. It unlocks fan funding and shopping but no ad revenue. — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en)
- UNVERIFIED: vidIQ says the thresholds rise to 8,000 watch hours or 20M Shorts views from Feb 1, 2027. I did not find this on Google's own pages. — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/pt/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-guide)
- On July 15, 2025, YouTube renamed its "repetitious content" policy to "inauthentic content". The policy covers mass-produced or repetitive content, such as templated videos with little variation or content that is "easily replicable at scale". If YouTube "cannot clearly tell that you made the content", it may remove monetization from the whole channel. — [YouTube Help 1311392](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2025/07/08/youtube-targets-inauthentic-content/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-clarifies-monetization-update-inauthentic-repeated-content/752892/)
- YouTube's examples of non-monetizable content: narrated stories with only superficial differences between them, and slideshows that all share the same narration. AI use alone does not make a channel ineligible. Realistic synthetic content must be disclosed. — [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/software-apps/youtube-ramps-up-campaign-against-ai-slop-with-stricter-monetization-caveats-on-inauthentic-and-repetitive-videos); [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/youtube-responds-to-ai-concerns/)
- July 20, 2026 (snippet only; full article blocked): YouTube reportedly split "inauthentic content" into three non-monetizable categories: generic or template-based content, off-putting or distressing content, and content where AI personas discuss topics. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/)
- January 2026 sweep: 16 channels with 35M combined subscribers and 4.7B views were hit, 11 terminated and 5 wiped, under YouTube's spam policies. Kapwing counted at least 18. — [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/youtube-cracks-down-ai-generated-content-1800213); [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/01/29/youtube-ai-slop-channel-crackdown-bans/amp/); [Futurism](https://futurism.com/artificial-intelligence/youtube-shutting-down-ai-slop-channels)
- A Google research paper, covered around July 2026, says an automated system terminated 50,000 clusters covering 130,000 channels in six months by targeting coordinated AI spam networks. — [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/artificial-intelligence/how-google-machine-terminated-130000-ai-slop-youtube-channels-six-months-903645)
- Collateral damage, June 2026: coverage says the ranking system treats a visible human host as a signal of authenticity, which hurts human-made faceless channels too. Some creators now hire stand-in hosts or paid actors (Semafor, via TNW). — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage); [TNW: ghost creators](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ghost-creators-paid-actors-ai-detection-loophole); [Hollywood Reporter](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/digital/faceless-creators-youtube-ai-damage-1236617586/)

### Inferences
- The classic "faceless AI shorts / Reddit-story / slideshow" playbook is now the very thing the policy names as ineligible. The risk is not just slow growth: a channel can be demonetized or terminated after it has already been built.
- Faceless channels with real editorial input, such as original research, scripting and distinct visuals, can still be monetized, but they face an algorithmic headwind against faceless content.

### Gaps
- I found no official count of channels demonetized under the inauthentic-content policy. The "thousands" figures are poorly sourced.
- I could not read the full text of the July 2026 TechCrunch article.

## TikTok Creator Rewards rates and AI labeling rules

### Takeaway
TikTok Creator Rewards pays roughly $0.40 to $1 per 1,000 qualified views (estimate). It requires 10k followers, 100k views in 30 days, age 18+ and videos of 1 minute or longer. Realistic AI content must be labeled. Secondary sources disagree on whether fully AI-generated videos can earn rewards at all.

### Cited Findings
- Commonly cited eligibility: 10k followers, 100k views in 30 days, age 18+, videos of at least 1 minute. — [Influencer Marketing Hub](https://influencermarketinghub.com/tiktok-creator-rewards-program/); [Daily Dot](https://www.dailydot.com/passionfruit/tiktok-creator-rewards-program)
- RPM is about $0.40 to $1 per 1,000 qualified views (third-party estimates). Reports describe falling RPM and pay cuts in 2026. — [Soundstripe](https://www.soundstripe.com/blogs/how-much-does-tiktok-pay-per-view); [Quasa](https://quasa.io/media/tiktok-creator-rewards-program-2026-strict-requirements-and-falling-rpm-explained); [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/tiktok-creators-rewards-programme-faces-drastic-pay-reductions-that-are-forcing-career-exit-plans-1773819)
- Creators must label realistic synthetic people, voices or scenes. Unlabeled content risks removal or an account flag. Unoriginal or duplicate content is excluded from rewards. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/ai-generated-videos-monetised-tiktok/); [Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-news/tiktok-cracks-down-on-ai-spam-so-real-creators-can-stand-out-more)
- CONFLICT: one vendor blog says fully AI-generated content is barred from Creator Rewards and describes escalating penalties ending in permanent removal after a 4th offense. Other sources say labeled, original AI-assisted content can still earn. — [Storrito](https://storrito.com/resources/what-tiktoks-ai-monetization-restrictions-signal-for-creator-income/) vs [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/ai-generated-videos-monetised-tiktok/)

### Inferences
- At a $0.40 to $1 RPM, 1M qualified views pays only about $400 to $1,000. Making meaningful income from TikTok payouts takes tens of millions of views a month.

### Gaps
- I found no official TikTok policy page in search results, so the ineligibility of fully AI-generated content is unconfirmed.

## Typical RPMs for faceless niches, and how many channels reach monetization

### Takeaway
Long-form RPMs are usually $3 to $6, with finance and education higher, around $15 to $18 or more. Shorts RPMs are usually $0.02 to $1.48, often under $0.20, which is 3% to 14% of long-form. I found no reliable public data on what share of new channels reach YPP.

### Cited Findings
- AIR Media-Tech, 274 channels, YouTube Analytics API data updated June 23, 2026: Shorts RPM runs $0.02 to $1.48, or 3% to 14% of long-form RPM. Music is the exception at 41% to 60%. The highest long-form RPM is education, at $14.97 (medium tier) to $18.23 (small tier). — [AIR: Shorts vs long-form](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-shorts-rpm-vs-long-form-how-much-do-shorts-earn-in-2026); [AIR: niche](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/what-is-youtube-shorts-rpm-in-your-niche-in-2026)
- Digiday creator interviews: Shorts RPM is consistently below $0.20, while long-form averages $3 to $6. — [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/how-youtube-shorts-revenue-compares-to-long-form-video-revenue-for-creators)
- Finance is often quoted at $15 to $40 and entertainment at $1 to $5. ESTIMATE, no stated methodology. — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/youtube-long-form-vs-shorts-earnings-2026); [vidIQ niches](https://www.vidiq.com/blog/post/most-profitable-youtube-niches)

### Inferences
- An AI Shorts channel at 10M views a month earns roughly $200 to $1,500 a month (estimate). The cheap-to-make format is also the lowest-paying one.

### Gaps
- I found no credible data on the percentage of new or faceless channels that reach monetization.

## Google helpful content / spam updates and AI-generated affiliate sites

### Takeaway
Google's scaled content abuse policy, together with the core and spam updates of 2024-2026 (including an August 2026 spam update), has repeatedly hit sites that publish AI content at volume. Google penalizes scale and low value, not AI use as such. The best traffic data comes from small studies.

### Cited Findings
- Lily Ray's analysis of 220 sites: 54% lost 30% or more of peak organic traffic after publishing AI content, and 39% lost 50% or more. Early gains rarely held, and the losses often exceeded the original peak. The sample is small. — [Neil Patel summary](https://neilpatel.com/marketing-stats/how-does-google-treat-ai-generated-content/); [SEJ](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-strategies-that-backfire/); [Lily Ray](https://lilyraynyc.substack.com/p/it-works-until-it-doesnt-ai-content-risks)
- Ahrefs, 331k pages: pages with low or moderate AI content got 2 to 3 times the impressions of pages with high AI content. This is correlation only. — [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/google-doesnt-punish-ai-content/)
- A Search Engine Land experiment and the August 2026 spam update case studies document AI affiliate sites losing visibility. Coverage also says Google's newer detector judges site networks, not individual pages. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-spam-update-ai-affiliate-sites-seo-experiment-470168); [GSQi Aug 2026](https://gsqi.com/marketing-blog/august-2026-google-spam-update-case-studies/); [CMSWire](https://www.cmswire.com/digital-experience/googles-new-ai-spam-detector-judges-networks-not-pages-high-ai-sites-are-already-slipping/)
- A claimed 40% to 70% loss for AI affiliate review sites is not traceable to any dataset, so treat it as weak. — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/scaled-content-abuse-google-march-update-ai-pages-decimated)

### Inferences
- Pumping out AI pages at scale for affiliate SEO now follows a "works until it doesn't" pattern. Expect the first dollar to take 6 to 12 months or more (estimate, from general SEO experience, not sourced) and the site to be at risk of collapse in a later update. AI Overviews cutting click-through is a separate headwind that I did not research in depth.

### Gaps
- I found no independently audited study isolating the cause. I also did not find solid data on AI Overviews' click-through impact on affiliate sites in this pass.

## Newsletter (Beehiiv / Substack) monetization data

### Takeaway
Newsletters are among the more durable models, but the typical earnings are small. Beehiiv's median paid conversion rate is 0.62%, and its ad network pays out more than $1M a month in total across a very large publisher base.

### Cited Findings
- Beehiiv State of Paid Newsletters 2026 (June 22, 2026): median paid conversion is 0.62%. Sports averages 1.93% and economy 1.28%. — [beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-paid-newsletters-2026.md)
- Beehiiv's ad network pays more than $1M a month to publishers. Beehiiv doubled its ad sales team in January 2026. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/beehiiv-doubles-ad-sales-team-newsletter-monetization-heats-up)
- Press Gazette (February 2026): Beehiiv's CEO said creators' collective annual earnings from subscriptions and ads are approaching $5M, excluding direct deals. Beehiiv keeps 5% to 20% of network ad revenue. Substack takes 10% of subscriptions. These are company statements. — [Press Gazette](https://pressgazette.co.uk/newsletters/newsletters-2026-prices-retention-churn/)
- Beehiiv State of Newsletters 2026, January 6, 2026. — [beehiiv](https://www.beehiiv.com/blog/the-state-of-newsletters-2026)

### Inferences
- At 0.62% conversion, 5,000 subscribers yields about 31 paying readers, or roughly $150 to $250 a month at $5 to $8 each (estimate). A purely AI-written newsletter has no differentiation, so these numbers likely sit at or below the median.

### Gaps
- I found no data on AI-written newsletters specifically. The "$5M" figure seems low against the "$1M a month" ad payout, so the two statements may be inconsistent or measure different things. I did not find Substack's own 2026 figures.

## AI UGC creators

### Takeaway
Human UGC rates are about $30 to $70 per video for new creators on Billo, with medians around $175 to $198 (estimate). Rates fell about 44% from 2024 to 2025. AI UGC tools such as Arcads (about $11 per video) compete directly with these creators, so "AI UGC creator" income is undocumented and under pressure.

### Cited Findings
- Billo: brands pay from $99 per video. Creator payouts for a 15-second video start at $30 (new) or $70 (premium), plus complexity bonuses of $20 to $190. — [Billo](https://billo.app/blog/ugc-rates/)
- Median per-video rate is about $175 to $198 after a roughly 44% drop from 2024 to 2025. ESTIMATE, from a vendor blog. — [FindClout](https://findclout.com/blog/ugc-creator-rates-2026); [Insense](https://insense.pro/blog/ugc-creator-rates)
- AI UGC tools: Arcads about $11 per video, Creatify from $49 a month. — [FluxNote](https://fluxnote.io/guides/how-much-do-ugc-ads-cost)

### Gaps
- I found no reliable earnings data for people who sell AI-generated UGC.

## Verified case studies vs. guru income claims

### Takeaway
The large earnings figures are mostly estimated from Social Blade data, not verified payouts. The most-cited case, Kapwing's top 10 AI slop channels at $33.6M a year, shows a winner-take-all pattern, and YouTube later removed several of those channels.

### Cited Findings
- Kapwing AI Slop Report (November 2025): of about 15,000 top trending channels, 278 posted only AI slop. In a fresh account's first 500 Shorts, 21% were AI slop and 33% were "brainrot". The top 10 slop channels earn an estimated $33.6M a year. The top channel, Bandar Apna Dost, has 2.07B views and an estimated $4.25M a year. All earnings are Social Blade estimates. — [Kapwing](https://www.kapwing.com/blog/ai-slop-report-the-global-rise-of-low-quality-ai-videos/); [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/2025/12/03/study-finds-ai-slop-videos-spreading-fast-across-youtube-and-theres-a-lot-of-money-being-made/)
- Critique: detected ads do not prove YPP membership or actual revenue, and no verified dollar figures exist. — [ContentGrip](https://www.contentgrip.com/faceless-youtube-income-evidence/) (snippet only)
- An anecdotal profile describes "$300k from sleep videos". It is single-source and unverified. — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/he-made-$300k-from-videos-people-fall-asleep-to-inside-youtubes-ai-slop-economy?source=rss)
- AI UGC vendors cite testimonials claiming $8,227 to $100,227 in earnings. These are unverifiable. — [search summary of vendor pages](https://www.imagine.art/blogs/how-to-make-money-with-ugc)

### Inferences
- Many guru "faceless channel" income claims point back to course sales. The verifiable evidence shows a few outliers, many channels never reaching monetization, and rising platform-removal risk.
- Startup costs (estimate): about $0 to $100 a month in AI tools for video, newsletter and blog formats. The real cost is time. For time to first dollar: YouTube depends on the YPP threshold and often takes months, TikTok needs 10k followers, newsletters need a list, and SEO sites take months. UGC is the fastest, paying per gig.

### Gaps
- I found no audited income disclosures from faceless AI channels or AI blog operators. Saturation is shown only indirectly, by the 21% slop share of Shorts and the enforcement scale.
